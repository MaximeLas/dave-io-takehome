# The `ids` rule (2026-10-11, commit `03a1f56`)

**Pattern.** The agent found what the key expects (mean recall about 90%) and claimed more (precision 61 to 74%). Of Haiku's 17 to 20 failures per run, 13 or 14 missed nothing and only added ids. The extras were of three kinds:
- the resources the question starts from: the four order queues listed beside their dead-letter queues, for "If messages in the production order queues keep failing, where do they end up?";
- the hops on the way: the instance profile, role and policy listed beside the two instances, for "Which compute instances can reach bucket-commerce-prod-1?";
- related context: the repository beside its workflow, for "Which repository's code ends up on i-ecomm-dev-02?".

**Hypothesis.** The field text ("every resource the answer asserts … every node on a path") reads as "everything the answer touches". A definition of what the question asks for removes the extras without losing recall.

**Change.** One rule (`IDS_RULE` in `src/agent/prompt.ts`), used as both a prompt paragraph and the schema field's description. The ids are the resources that answer what the question asks for, of that kind; not the starting point, not the hops, not context. A path question's path is its answer. The list is empty on "none".

**Moved.** Fifteen questions gained passes.

**Regressed.** "What is the compute stack in my dev env?" went from 2 of 3 runs to 1 of 3: twice it dropped the load balancer and its target groups, reading "of the kind asked" as instances only.

**Prose.** Still explains the chain and the checks, about 9% shorter.

**Verdict:** kept.
